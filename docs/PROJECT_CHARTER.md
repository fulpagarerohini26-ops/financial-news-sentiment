# Project Charter

## Project Title
News Sentiment Analysis and Stock Market Trend Exploration

## Phase
Phase 1 – Discovery and Scope

## Purpose
Develop a data-driven application that analyzes financial news sentiment for publicly traded companies and explores how sentiment trends align with stock-price movements over time.

## Problem Statement
Investors, analysts, and learners consume large volumes of financial news but often lack an efficient way to quantify market sentiment and observe potential relationships between news sentiment and stock performance.

## Target Users
- Financial analysts exploring company-specific market sentiment
- Learners studying financial data analytics, NLP, and market behavior

## Inputs
- Company ticker symbol (e.g., AAPL, MSFT, TSLA)
- User-defined date range
- Financial news headlines
- Available article summaries or related news metadata

## Outputs
- Sentiment labels (Positive, Neutral, Negative)
- Sentiment confidence scores
- Sentiment trend visualizations and charts
- Exploratory comparison between sentiment trends and stock-price movements

## Scope
### In Scope
- Collection and processing of financial news data
- Sentiment analysis using NLP techniques
- Time-series visualization of sentiment trends
- Comparison of sentiment trends with historical stock-price data
- Interactive analysis for selected companies and date ranges

### Out of Scope
- Investment recommendations
- Automated trading decisions
- Financial forecasting or price prediction
- Real-time trading execution

## Key Assumption
The availability of reliable financial news headlines, summaries, and historical stock-price data for the selected companies.

## Limitation
Correlation between sentiment and stock-price movements does not establish causation and should not be interpreted as a guarantee of future returns.

## Success Criteria
- Users can analyze sentiment for a selected company and time period.
- Sentiment results are displayed with clear labels and scores.
- Trend charts effectively visualize sentiment changes over time.
- Historical stock-price movements can be compared with sentiment trends for exploratory analysis.
